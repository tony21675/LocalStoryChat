The late afternoon sun cast long shadows down the front road as Tiffany and Maya continued their easy walk. The sound of their footsteps hitting the pavement mixed with the occasional distant hum of traffic—the rhythm of a normal day winding down.

“Seriously though,” Tiffany said, kicking a stray pebble that skittered across the sidewalk, “did Ms. Davison even grade those essays? Because I swear Mr. Harrison gave us an A on our last one.”

Maya laughed, a bright, airy sound that seemed perfectly matched to the relaxed mood between them. She adjusted the strap of her backpack, her eyes briefly glancing up at the houses flanking the street before returning to Tiffany. “Don’t ask about grading right now. We need to focus on surviving until we get to the couch.”

“Speaking of survival,” Tiffany teased, nudging Maya lightly with her elbow, “are you sure your mom isn't going to have you doing chores until Christmas? You seem suspiciously carefree today.”

“Hey!” Maya protested, although she didn’t sound genuinely offended. “I feel pretty productive today. Plus, my parents are surprisingly chill when it comes to me walking home after school.” She paused, considering the quiet stretch ahead. “It’s nice, though. Just walking this way. It feels… peaceful.”

Tiffany slowed her pace slightly, matching Maya’s stride. The conversation drifted easily from school logistics to trivial observations about their neighborhood—the overgrown rose bushes, the neighbor’s perpetually barking terrier, and the general state of local lawn care.

“Yeah,” Tiffany murmured, kicking another pebble. “It is peaceful. Though I do wonder what Tony is up to in there.” She gestured vaguely back toward the direction of their shared house.

Maya followed the thought, a small smile touching her lips. “Probably passed out again. He looked exhausted yesterday after that… thing.”

“He just needs his rest,” Tiffany said softly, perhaps a touch too quickly. She glanced down at the dog tags nestled against her own sweater before continuing, “It was a long week for him, even if he won't admit it.”

They fell into a comfortable silence punctuated only by their footsteps and the whisper of the late afternoon breeze through the leaves overhead. They were nearing the curve where the road started to bend closer to the corner leading to Maya’s property, but they hadn't reached the familiar sight of the curb or the entrance path yet.
